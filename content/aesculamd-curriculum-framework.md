# AesculaMD Curriculum Framework
### How every stage maps to real physician-readiness, not resume-building

---

## The core idea

Every existing premed resource — Reddit, consultants, most advising offices — optimizes for "what looks good on an application." That produces students who can describe the right activities but can't always explain, under real interview pressure, *why those activities changed them.* Admissions committees notice the difference immediately, and so do patients later.

The AAMC already solved this problem for us. In 2011, and again in a 2023 refresh, medical schools jointly built a set of **17 Premed Competencies** — not as a checklist of activities, but as a description of the actual traits that predict success as a *physician*, which holistic-review committees are trained to look for across every part of an application. This is the framework AesculaMD builds around. Every stage in the bootcamp exists to develop specific, named competencies — genuinely — not to manufacture the appearance of them.

The distinction we're building toward: a checkbox-built applicant can list their activities. A competency-built applicant can tell you, unprompted, what they learned about themselves doing it — and that's the difference between an application that reads as authentic and one that reads as performed.

---

## The 17 Premed Competencies (AAMC, current framework)

**Professional Competencies (11)**
1. Commitment to Learning and Growth — pursuing feedback and improvement deliberately, not just accumulating hours
2. Empathy and Compassion
3. Ethical Responsibility to Self and Others
4. Interpersonal Skills
5. Oral Communication
6. Reliability and Dependability
7. Resilience and Adaptability
8. Self-Awareness — knowing your own limitations, biases, and growth edges
9. Service Orientation
10. Teamwork and Collaboration
11. Understanding Others — genuine engagement with people whose backgrounds differ from your own

**Science Competencies (2)**
12. Human Behavior
13. Living Systems

**Thinking & Reasoning Competencies (4)**
14. Critical Thinking
15. Quantitative Reasoning
16. Scientific Inquiry
17. Written Communication

*(Definitions paraphrased from AAMC's official framework; full definitions live at students-residents.aamc.org for content-writing reference.)*

Importantly: **one real experience typically builds several competencies at once.** A single semester volunteering at a free clinic might genuinely build Service Orientation, Understanding Others, and Resilience simultaneously — the goal of our reflection prompts is to help a student see and articulate all of that, not just log the hours.

## Stage-by-stage competency map

| Stage | Primary competencies built | What "genuine" looks like vs. the checkbox version |
|---|---|---|
| **01 · Premed 101** | Self-Awareness, Commitment to Learning and Growth | Checkbox version: memorizing the timeline. Genuine version: an honest first assessment of which competencies a student already has evidence for, and which are unfamiliar — this becomes the seed of their personalized roadmap. |
| **02 · Be Yourself: Narrative** | Self-Awareness, Ethical Responsibility to Self and Others | Checkbox version: a generic "why medicine" essay draft. Genuine version: identifying the real formative experience(s) driving their interest, named honestly, revisited and refined over years rather than invented under deadline pressure senior year. |
| **03 · Cost & Access** *(not an AAMC competency — our addition; sequenced here, before Strategy, since a real budget picture should inform that decision, not follow it)* | Reliability and Dependability, Ethical Responsibility to Self and Others (financial planning as a form of self-responsibility) | Genuine version: a student who has planned realistically rather than being blindsided mid-cycle — confidence that comes from preparedness, not from ignoring the problem. |
| **04 · Strategy (MD/DO)** | Self-Awareness, Critical Thinking | Checkbox version: picking MD because it's the "default." Genuine version: an honest match between the student's own values/constraints and what each path actually requires and offers. |
| **05 · Grades & MCAT** | Quantitative Reasoning, Scientific Inquiry, Commitment to Learning and Growth, Human Behavior, Living Systems | Checkbox version: cramming. Genuine version: building real study systems and intellectual habits that will still matter in medical school itself, not just for one exam — and the one stage where the two Science competencies get demonstrated through direct performance rather than reflection. |
| **06 · Clinical Experience** | Service Orientation, Understanding Others, Empathy and Compassion, Interpersonal Skills | Checkbox version: hour-collecting. Genuine version: a student who can articulate a specific moment that changed how they think about patients, sickness, or care. |
| **07 · Volunteering & Compassion** | Service Orientation, Empathy and Compassion, Understanding Others | Checkbox version: service chosen for optics. Genuine version: service connected to the student's own stated "why," sustained rather than one-off. |
| **08 · Research or No Research?** | Scientific Inquiry, Critical Thinking, Written Communication, Commitment to Learning and Growth | Checkbox version: listing a technique ("ran Western blots"). Genuine version: the student can explain *why the question mattered* and what they learned about the scientific process itself, even from a failed experiment. |
| **09 · Leadership** | Teamwork and Collaboration, Interpersonal Skills, Reliability and Dependability | Checkbox version: a title with no substance. Genuine version: a specific instance of navigating conflict, motivating others, or being accountable for something that mattered. |
| **10 · Personal Brand / Being Unique / LORs** | Self-Awareness, Written Communication, Oral Communication | Checkbox version: a generic, polished-sounding profile. Genuine version: consistency between how the student talks about themselves across essays, interviews, and what their letter-writers actually know about them. |

Two competencies — **Resilience and Adaptability** and **Interpersonal Skills** — are deliberately not owned by one stage. They should be built cumulatively through reflection prompts across *every* stage, since real resilience shows up in how a student responds when a plan changes, not in a single dedicated unit.

## How this powers the AI-generated, personalized roadmap

This mapping is what turns "one curriculum for everyone" into "a roadmap unique to each student" — the distinction you're building toward:

1. **Stage 01 doubles as a real self-assessment**, modeled on AAMC's own self-assessment approach: rather than guessing, the student honestly rates which competencies they already have some evidence for (from life before college, not just premed activities) and which are unfamiliar.
2. **The roadmap generator weights the remaining stages based on that self-assessment** — a student who already has strong Service Orientation and Understanding Others from years of caregiving or community work doesn't need the same emphasis there as a student starting from zero; their time gets redirected toward genuinely underdeveloped areas instead.
3. **Every logged hour and reflection gets tagged to the competencies it plausibly builds**, not just an activity category — so a student's dashboard over time shows a real, evidence-backed picture of which of the 17 they can speak to concretely, which is exactly the structure AAMC's own "Anatomy of an Applicant" resource uses with real accepted students.
4. **This is also the actual confidence mechanism.** A student walking into an interview senior year isn't reciting a memorized narrative — they're drawing on two-plus years of dated, specific, self-recognized evidence of real growth. That's a categorically different, and more durable, kind of confidence than a well-rehearsed pitch.

---

## What this means for content-writing going forward

Every future stage module should open by naming which competencies it targets, in plain language the student will actually internalize — not buried in a compliance footnote. And every reflection prompt should be written to produce evidence for the tracker, not just a diary entry: specific enough that six months later, the student (or an admissions committee) could read it and see the competency demonstrated, not just described.
